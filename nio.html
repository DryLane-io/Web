Nio

Zero-copy messaging. 16 µs round-trips, and hundreds of millions of messages a second on a handful of threads.

16 µs round-trip, median. gRPC takes 179 µs on the same machine. 206M messages a second across 64 connections, on 12 threads. 2.67 GiB/s bulk stream throughput. Raw sockets reach 2.34. 12 MB of thread memory at 1,000 connections. Thread-per-connection needs 2 GB.

Every figure here goes through the C# wrapper. The native core underneath is faster.

What it is
The network layer the rest of the stack rides on. Messages go from one buffer to the wire and back without copies: tens of millions a second on one connection, hundreds of millions fanned out.
The problem
Most systems pay a tax on every message: serialize, copy into the kernel, copy out, deserialize. Nio deletes the copies, so a round-trip takes microseconds instead of milliseconds.
For example
It carries replication in the failover demo, fast enough that the standby stays within one unsealed segment of the leader.

The crowd test: 64 connections
12 threads. 206M msg/s. Thread-per-connection needs 128 threads, a pair per connection, to push the same load. Nio drains all 64 connections with a few poll threads and no locks, doing 7 times the work per thread.

Scaling to 1,000 connections

Thread-per-connection wins the drag race and loses the war. Here is the same test at 1,000 connections:

1,000 connectionsAggregateOS threadsThread memory1 s push took
Nio130M msg/s12~12 MB1.0 s
raw thread-per-conn307M msg/s2,000~2 GB7.2 s
Flat threads, flat memory. Nio runs a fixed pool of poll threads over pre-allocated, GC-free buffers, the same at 64 connections or 10,000. Thread-per-connection pays about 1 MB of stack per thread: at 1,000 connections that's 2,000 threads and 2 GB of stacks before a byte of payload, and the scheduler buckles. A one-second push took seven.

Test bed: AMD Ryzen 9 9950X (16 cores, 32 threads), 64 GB RAM, Windows 11 Pro, .NET 10 Release x64, loopback, through the C# wrapper. 64-byte messages; throughput is time-boxed, median of repeated runs. "Raw thread-per-conn" is one OS thread per socket per side, the plain blocking model. Full harness in samples/Nio.Benchmarks.
