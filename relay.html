Relay

Pub/sub where the log is the broker. Publishing is an append, replay is a scan and delivery takes microseconds.

35× faster history replay than NATS JetStream: 50M against 1.3M msg/s. 4M msg/s durable pub/sub, persisted. JetStream manages 0.15M. 1–3 µs from publish to handler, median. NATS takes about 11. 3–7 µs request/reply round trip, median. NATS takes about 20.

A JetStream publish-ack is a stronger per-message promise than Relay's append with periodic flush, so part of the durable gap is semantics. The full footnotes sit under the table.

What it is
Messaging fused onto Facts. A durable subject is a stream in the column store, so publishing a message and persisting it are the same operation. No broker process, no second system for history. Subscribers ride live pushes, and anyone who falls behind or joins late reads the log directly.
The problem
Brokered messaging pays twice: every live message crosses to a server and back, and every replay is a stream of per-message round trips. In Relay, catching up is a columnar scan at tens of millions of messages a second.
Patterns
Request/reply and streaming responses are a 16-byte convention on top of pub/sub, an inbox and a correlation id, not a bolted-on RPC runtime. Across machines, nodes form a full mesh over Nio, and a consumer that misses messages heals itself from the origin's log, exactly once and in order.
Measured against NATS

NATS is the closest rival in design: subjects, request/reply and durable streams through JetStream. Same box, 16-byte messages, settings that favour NATS (separate pub and sub connections, pipelined publishes, file-backed JetStream with a deep ack window), measured as sustained delivered messages with identical pacing on both sides.

Replay at 50M msg/s, about 35 times JetStream. History isn't a fetch, it's a scan. A consumer replaying two million persisted messages reads the column store directly, with no broker in the read path. That's what "the log is the broker" buys.

The embedded column compares designs, not wire speed: Relay lives inside your process, and NATS always crosses to a server. That gap is the product. NATS is fast and battle-tested, and where it fits, it fits. Relay's bet is that most systems don't need a separate broker and pay dearly for having one.

What a subscriber gets
  • Live, from an offset, or full replay. The seam between catch-up and live is exactly-once and in order, with no dedup pass, because push sequence numbers and log positions are the same number.
  • A slow consumer never stalls a publisher. On overflow the subscription reads the log at its own pace, then re-attaches. Kafka semantics, per subscriber, for free.
  • Wildcards (home.chat.*) resolve at subscribe time, and a subject created later splices in before its first message can be missed.
  • Cross-node: a full mesh over Nio with credit-based flow control that pauses instead of dropping, messages fragmented up to 1 MiB, and gap repair that fetches straight from the origin's log.
  • Failover comes from the storage layer. Durable subjects are Facts streams, so a leader can die and the consumer keeps delivering the next message, with no second consensus system inside the messaging tier.
When to use NATS or Kafka instead

Relay is embedded and .NET-first. It wins by deleting the broker, which also defines where it doesn't fit:

  • You need clients in many languages today. Relay speaks C# now; other languages come through the documented wire protocol and the gateway.
  • You need dynamic fleets with hundreds of nodes joining and leaving. Relay's mesh is a deliberately static full mesh of up to 64 nodes.
  • You need consumer groups now. The partition primitive exists; group coordination is on the roadmap.
  • You need multi-tenancy and a hardened security model in the messaging tier, or a decade of operational track record. NATS and Kafka have both. Relay has neither yet.
Where it fits

Zao packs the message, Relay routes and persists it, Facts is the log it lives in and Nio carries it between machines. The same bytes go from your struct to the wire to the column store, with no seams.
